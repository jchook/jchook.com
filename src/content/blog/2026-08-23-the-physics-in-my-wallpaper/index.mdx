My wallpaper is six rounded bars on a black field. People assume it's abstract
art. It's not — it's the **emission spectrum of hydrogen**, remixed into
gruvbox. Every time an electron in a hydrogen atom falls from a higher orbit
down to the second one, it spits out a photon at one of a handful of exact
wavelengths. Those are the bars.

The wavelengths come from the Rydberg formula:

$$
\frac{1}{\lambda} = R_H \left( \frac{1}{2^2} - \frac{1}{n^2} \right),
\qquad n = 3, 4, 5, \ldots
$$

with $R_H \approx 1.097 \times 10^7\,\text{m}^{-1}$. Plug in the first few
values of $n$ and you get the visible lines — the **Balmer series**:

| transition | name | $\lambda$ |
| ---------- | ---- | --------- |
| $3 \to 2$  | H-α  | 656 nm (red) |
| $4 \to 2$  | H-β  | 486 nm (aqua-ish) |
| $5 \to 2$  | H-γ  | 434 nm (blue) |
| $6 \to 2$  | H-δ  | 410 nm (violet) |

Here's the whole mechanism, gruvboxed. Watch the electron fall to $n = 2$ and
fire off the corresponding line:

The $\frac{1}{2^2}$ is the whole story of why this series is *visible*.
Transitions down to $n = 1$ (the Lyman series) are ultraviolet; down to
$n = 3$ (Paschen) is infrared. Landing on the second shell happens to drop
photons right into the narrow band our eyes evolved to catch.
